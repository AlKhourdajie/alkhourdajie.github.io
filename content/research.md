:::section 1
:::quote
One's discipline is like a pair of prescription glasses: it brings certain things into sharp focus and makes them legible. But clarity is not the same as completeness: the prescription corrects for some distortions while introducing others. To venture into another discipline is to take off a long-worn pair and try on lenses ground to a different specification, one that may, at first, make the familiar blurry even as it reveals what was previously invisible. The temptation is to keep switching back to the comfortable pair. The real work begins when you learn to tolerate the discomfort long enough to see what the new prescription is actually showing you. And the most productive interdisciplinary work comes not from choosing one pair over the other, but from understanding what each corrects for, what each distorts, and when each one is most needed.
:::
